# New Zealand IRD IR3 and IR4 Income Tax and GST Filing Guide

As a tax preparer and a US tax preparer with a focus on cross-border tax compliance, Abdullah Tax is dedicated to helping New Zealand individuals, contractors, and companies navigate the complexities of income tax and GST (Goods and Services Tax) filing. This guide aims to simplify the process, ensuring you understand the key deadlines, rates, and penalties associated with IRD IR3 and IR4.

## Understanding IRD IR3 and IR4

IRD IR3 and IR4 are specific tax codes used by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of New Zealand. IRD IR3 is for individuals and IRD IR4 is for companies. They are part of the broader IRD tax system and are used for income tax and GST calculations.

### Key Points to Remember

- **Deadline for IRD IR3 and IR4**: The IRD IR3 and IR4 filing deadlines are typically in December of the year following the tax year. However, it's important to check the specific deadlines provided by the IRD.
- **Income Tax Rates**: The income tax rates in New Zealand are progressive and vary based on your taxable income. For the latest rates, refer to the IRD's official tax rates guide.
- **GST Rates**: The GST rate in New Zealand is 15%, but it can be reduced to 5% for certain goods and services. For more details, consult the IRD's GST guide.
- **Penalties for Late Filing**: Late filing penalties can vary but generally, they are in the range of 5% to 10% of the unpaid tax for each month or part of a month the return is late. For IRD IR3 and IR4, the penalties can be higher and it's important to file on time.

## Practical Checklist for Filing IRD IR3 and IR4

### Step 1: Gather Your Tax Information
- **Income Statements**: Gather all income statements, including salaries, dividends, and any other sources of income.
- **Expenses**: Keep records of all business and personal expenses.
- **Tax Deductions**: Identify any eligible tax deductions you might be entitled to.

### Step 2: Calculate Your Tax Liability
- **Income**: Add up all your taxable income sources.
- **Expenses**: Subtract your eligible expenses from your total income.
- **Tax Deductions**: Apply any eligible tax deductions to your total income.
- **Tax Rate**: Apply the appropriate tax rate based on your income level.
